# KurenaiOS: the whole app, in plain words

*A tour for anyone who wants to know what KurenaiOS is, what it does and how it
works. No technical knowledge needed. Last updated 24 October 2026.*

---

## 1. What it is

KurenaiOS is one personal website that replaces a pile of separate tools. It is
where a student **studies**, **plans**, **focuses**, **keeps track of money and
goals**, **learns to code**, **learns Japanese** and **keeps their anime, books,
visual novels and games**. All of it sits in one place, and the parts talk to
each other.

It was built for one A-level student (Computer Science, Mathematics and IT), so
it knows the real exam courses topic by topic. But the idea works for anyone:
a calm, private workspace that tells you what to do next, remembers everything
for you, and turns steady effort into visible progress.

You open it in a browser, or install it on a phone, tablet or computer like an
app. It lives at **kurenai-os.pages.dev**.

### Five ideas that run through everything

1. **Yours first.** Everything is saved on your own device the moment you do
   it. You do not need an account, and the app works without internet. Signing
   in is optional and only adds syncing between devices.
2. **One record, many views.** A deadline is entered once and then shows up on
   the calendar, in the countdowns, on Home and in your week's plan. Nothing is
   copied, so nothing can disagree with itself.
3. **Honest numbers.** A chart appears only when there is enough information to
   say something true. Otherwise the app says "not enough yet" instead of
   guessing. Figures you see are worked out from what you really did, never
   typed in by hand.
4. **Nothing you need is ever locked.** Notes, flashcards, quizzes, exam
   questions and the Focus timer are always available. The reward system
   (below) never stands between you and revision.
5. **It asks "what next?"** Instead of making you decide what to open, Home
   looks at your deadlines, exams, cards due and plan, and suggests the one
   thing that matters most right now.

## 2. A tour of the app

The left-hand side has the main sections: **Home**, **Study**, **Productivity**,
**Collection** and **Archive**. There is a search box that finds things across
everything you own, a bell for notifications, and the **Kurenai Assistant**.
On a phone, the sections become a bar at the bottom.

### Home: what should I do now?

Home is a dashboard that answers one question. It shows:

- **Up next**, **Today** and **Upcoming**: the thing most worth doing, today's
  lessons, events and tasks, and what is coming.
- A **Focus card** that suggests a study session. It knows when you have a
  study block booked, when an exam is close, or when an assignment is due.
- **Study hours**, the week's plan, and your latest **report** (see Reports).
- A switch to see your **Goals**.

Empty parts stay small. The page never fills space just to look busy.

### Study

**The courses.** The full official lists of topics for three courses are built
in, down to every single point the exam board lists (331 in all): AQA Computer
Science, Edexcel Mathematics (Pure, Statistics and Mechanics) and the OCR IT
units being taken. These form the "spine" of the app: pick any topic and you
land on its page.

**A topic page** has everything for that point in one place:

- **Notes** written in depth, with diagrams, worked examples, memory tricks
  and warnings about common mistakes.
- **Flashcards**, a **quiz** and **exam questions** with mark schemes, in the
  style of the real papers.
- **Simulations**: interactive demonstrations you can play with (sorting
  algorithms, graphs, number systems, logic circuits, probability plots and
  many more).
- A side panel that records how confident you feel, what you have done on the
  topic and how well you remember it.
- **Your own notes and files.** You can edit the notes yourself, drag blocks
  into a new order, jot a quick note that files itself, and attach your own
  documents. Your edits never overwrite the original: "Reset" brings it back.

**Review: remembering for good.** Flashcards come back on a schedule, timed so
you see a card just before you would forget it. This uses a modern
memory-science method (FSRS) that learns from how you do. After enough
reviews it can tune itself to *you*. A card you keep forgetting is set aside
for extra help with a link to its notes. Before an exam, cards that would be
due after the exam date are brought forward so that you are sharp on the day.

**Assignments.** Homework and coursework with due dates, estimates, linked
topics and a tick when done.

**Exams & Deadlines.** One place for every exam, mock, topic test and deadline:

- You say what an exam **covers**, and the app works out how ready you are.
- **Readiness** comes from four separate signals and is never blended into one
  mystery score: how much you have covered, how well you remember it, how
  your timed practice is going, and how well you predict your own marks. It
  says "On track", "At risk" or "Thin (not enough information yet)".
- A **weekly plan** suggests what to revise and can send it to your plan, but
  only when you say so.
- A **past-paper library** lists every past question by topic and links it to
  your own files. You can log a paper question by question, tag why marks were
  lost and mark a question "redo" so it comes back as a card.
- **Exam Season** switches on automatically in the four weeks before a real
  exam: calmer notifications and a focus on what matters.
- For **Computer Science Paper 2**, a complete **Companion**: every type of
  exam question explained, worked several ways with the marks shown, common
  traps from the examiners' own reports, practice sets and predicted papers.
  All of it is original, written for this app. Real exam questions appear only
  as references. More papers are on the way.

**Pacing.** The school's term plan and your own personal plan, side by side,
week by week. Each lesson can be ticked. Unfinished personal items carry over
automatically and show how far behind they are. A **braid** diagram shows,
subject by subject, where the school plan and your own plan meet and where
class teaches something you have not planned to cover.

**Code and Japanese** are also study subjects. They are described below.

### Productivity

**Focus.** A study timer in four styles: Pomodoro, custom length, stopwatch and
"study until" a set time. You can switch subject mid-session without stopping
the clock. If you step away for half an hour, it warns you, then ends the
session and counts only the time you really worked. Reload the page and your
session is still there, paused. A short review afterwards lets you note how it
went.

**Calendar.** Events, repeats and exams. Bills, income and planner chips
appear on it too, but as read-only reminders drawn from their real home, not
copies. Each event can have its own alerts.

**Reminders and Habits.** Quick tasks with due times, and daily or weekly habits
with streaks.

**Money.** A personal budget that stays on your device:

- Categories with monthly budgets, and a pace marker that tells you whether
  you are ahead or behind.
- **Recurring bills, income and subscriptions**, including free trials that are
  about to turn into payments.
- **Pots** for saving towards things.
- Transactions, with CSV import and export.
- A "safe to spend" figure, shown only when the app has the evidence to work it
  out. A **Hide amounts** switch blurs every figure everywhere.

**Goals.** Set a target and the app measures it by reading your real activity:
hours studied, cards reviewed, topics turned green, a grade in an exam,
a savings pot, a Code lesson, a Collection target. Goals can repeat weekly
or monthly. A completed goal is recorded once and never pays out twice.

### The reward system (the "Governor")

Studying earns **XP**, **gold** and **HP**, and builds a **streak**. This is a
light game layer, with strict rules so it can never become a source of stress:

- Everything flows from a single honest record of sessions. Nothing can be
  typed in.
- **XP and gold** come from real work: finishing a focus session, passing a
  coding task, completing a lesson. Longer sessions pay more.
- **Gold** buys things in the shop: unlocking labs and simulations, and
  **themes** to restyle the app.
- **HP** is a wellbeing signal. It drains if you do nothing for days and
  recovers with a checklist. **HP never locks anything.**
- A **rest streak** counts time spent on leisure, so downtime is encouraged
  rather than penalised.

### Reports: your week, month, term and year

Each week is saved automatically on Monday morning, with a month, term or year
report on its own morning. A report shows:

- study time and topics, Focus blocks, the plan against what happened;
- what moved in the Collection, money and goal results, coding progress;
- what you earned and your streaks.

You can read it as a page, **play it as a short story** (like a social
"year in review" you tap through), turn the words into a **Kurenai-written**
summary or share a **picture card**. Written summaries use only totals, never
your notes, card text or purchases, and "What is sent" shows you the exact
message before anything leaves the device. Share cards leave out your
Collection titles unless you choose to include them.

### Code: learn C# from nothing to real projects

A complete programming course, treated as another study subject.

- **A core path of twelve units**, from first lines to classes, interfaces,
  LINQ, testing, asynchronous code and data structures. Four **capstone
  projects** build a text adventure, a shop, a revision planner and more.
- **Tracks** add depth. Track 1 covers databases (SQL), connecting to them
  from C#, web requests and building a web API. Track 2 builds real desktop
  apps.
- **Tasks run in the app.** You type code, press run and the app tells you
  whether it passes. It explains compile errors in plain words and gives
  hints. Your code runs only on your own device, in a sealed box with no
  internet and no access to your data.
- Early units use a light built-in runner. Later ones use an optional
  download of the real .NET compiler, kept for offline use.
- **Bigger projects use a real programming environment (Rider).** The app
  gives you the starter files and a checker. You run the checker on your
  computer and bring the result back; the app confirms it is genuine before
  counting it. The checker even plants bugs to prove your tests catch them.
- **Honest progress.** A lesson counts as done only when you have read every
  page, run every example and passed a task. Reading alone never completes a
  lesson. Each task pays once. Looking at the answer pays nothing and proves
  nothing.
- A **Daily 20** suggests twenty minutes of the most useful work, **katas**
  give short practice sets and **retrieval cards** bring old skills back
  before they fade.
- Optionally, your finished work can be saved to **your own GitHub**
  repository. The app never creates or deletes a repository, and a safety
  check blocks anything that looks like a secret.

### Japanese: from zero to reading and listening

A fifth study subject, designed for someone who starts knowing no Japanese.

- **Kana first.** Sixteen lessons teach hiragana and katakana with
  stroke-order guidance. Your handwriting is checked against the correct
  strokes.
- **Then real conversations.** Lessons are built as short **visual-novel
  scenes** and **manga panels** with a recurring cast, so you meet words and
  grammar inside a story. Every line is **voiced**. Unit 1 covers first
  conversations; unit 2 covers days, times, dates, trains and phone numbers.
  Further units are being written one at a time.
- **Four skills, practised separately.** Each word or grammar point is
  scheduled for **reading**, **listening**, **writing** and **speaking** on
  its own, because you can read a word and still not hear it. They open in
  order: reading and listening first, then writing and speaking.
- **Rule-based checking, never AI-marked.** Answers get one of four verdicts
  (correct, accepted, close or wrong) with an explanation of what went wrong,
  such as a missing small っ or a long vowel.
- **Progress you can see.** A skills radar, checkpoints at the end of each
  unit and a timeline that is shown as a *range* of likely dates, never a
  false promise of one.
- **Furigana and your own media.** Readings appear above the characters, and
  a line you save from your own media stays on your device.

### Collection: your anime, books, visual novels and games

A library for everything you watch, read and play.

- **Four vaults:** Anime, Books (digital and physical shelf), Visual Novels
  and Games.
- **Connected to the real services.** Anime and digital Books mirror your
  **AniList** lists; visual novels connect to **VNDB**. Changes in status,
  progress and score can flow back. Games can be searched and, with your
  approval, imported from Steam. The app never changes your reviews or
  favourites on those sites.
- **Your own layer on top:** notes, tags, moods, shelves, custom lists and
  cover positioning that never touches the original data.
- **Overview** summarises what is in progress and what airs this season.
- **Shrine:** a hall of fame for your best-loved titles, with your own order
  among titles that tie on score.
- **Wishlist:** things you plan to buy or get, with release days and a budget,
  and a "mark purchased" step that records the purchase in Money.

### The Kurenai Assistant

A chat helper with a character, available anywhere in the app. It can:

- answer questions about your own data (what is due, how a topic is going,
  where money went);
- **take actions for you**, such as adding a reminder, navigating to a page or
  editing a topic's notes. Anything that changes your study material asks for
  approval first, and most changes can be undone;
- send **scheduled messages**, a prompt of your choosing at a set time, such
  as a morning briefing;
- remember things about you, but only with your explicit consent.

It works with Gemini online or with a model on your own computer (Ollama). It
cannot see passwords, sign-in details or its own logs, and it has no way to
award XP or mark Code work as done: progress is earned in the app.

---

### Archive

- **Notifications:** one feed of things that happened to you (an alert, a bill,
  a deadline, a new report, an episode airing). You can mute whole kinds, set
  quiet hours and choose device alerts. With an account, important ones can
  reach you even when the app is closed.
- **Backup & Restore:** one file holds your whole workspace. Passwords and
  sign-in details are never included.
- **Help & Guide:** searchable instructions inside the app.

## 3. How it all works underneath (still in plain words)

**Where your data lives.** On your own device, in the browser's storage.
That is why it is fast and private, and why it works offline. Different
browsers or addresses keep separate copies, so Backup & Restore moves
everything between them.

**Syncing between devices (optional).** Sign in and your devices share one
account. Changes merge in the way a shared document does: two devices that both
add something keep both additions, and when the same item is edited on both, the
later edit wins field by field. The app never stops to ask you which copy to
keep, and an empty device can never wipe a full one.

**Privacy.** Search covers your study and organisation data but never passwords,
sign-ins, logs or assistant history. Nothing is uploaded unless you turn on a
feature that needs it (sync, the Assistant, a Kurenai-written report, a
GitHub push). Learner code runs in a sealed sandbox. Past exam papers are never
stored on the website; the app keeps only references to files on your own
computer.

**It works like an app.** It can be installed on a phone, tablet or computer
and runs offline. Updates arrive only after you confirm them, so the app never
changes under you mid-task. There is a dedicated layout for phone and for
iPad.

**It looks the way you like.** The default look, **Graphite**, is dark and
calm. **Dawn** is its light twin, the app can follow your device's light or
dark setting, and shop themes restyle the colours without changing anything
else. Every screen is built to be read with a keyboard and a screen reader.

## 4. A day with KurenaiOS

1. **Morning.** Home shows today's lessons, the one thing to do next and
   anything due. A notification says your weekly report is ready.
2. **Revision.** You open Review. Cards come up just as you are about to forget
   them. A topic page has the notes, a simulation and a quiz.
3. **Focus.** You start a 50-minute session on a topic. XP and gold are paid
   when it ends, honestly, for the time you really worked.
4. **After school.** You tick off the week's lesson in Pacing. An unfinished
   item rolls into next week by itself.
5. **Code or Japanese.** Twenty minutes: a coding task is checked and paid once;
   a Japanese scene is read, heard and answered.
6. **Evening.** You log a past-paper attempt, tag the mistakes and mark one
   question to redo, and it returns as a card tomorrow. You add a purchase to
   Money and check a goal.
7. **Weekend.** You watch an episode and update the progress in Collection.
   On Monday, a report summarises all of it.
