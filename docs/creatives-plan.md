# Creatives — a study space for art, design, editing and writing (plan, revision 2)

Roadmap 5.7 item 3, asked for on 20 October 2026 and planned on 22 October
2026, the day the iPad Air arrived. It follows the Code plan's shape: a
plan first, then Claude Design ([prompt 15](design-prompts.md)), then a
platform release and one track at a time.

**Revision 2 (22 October 2026)** follows your answers (§12) and approves
decisions A–G with three adjustments.
- **Beginner first.** You are starting from zero in all four tracks, and
  the risk is the taste gap: comparing Day 1 with finished professional
  work and stopping. Four mechanics are built in to answer it:
  - micro-studies before pieces (§3.1);
  - the Practice Log kept apart from the Portfolio, with Momentum mode
    and best-of-five (§4);
  - "Good for this unit" calibration in every rubric, and a critique that
    grades only the unit's skill (§3.3, §7);
  - a procedural reference generator, so a drill needs no files (§5.4).
- **Free first.** No paid app or subscription is needed until a track has
  proven itself. The in-app sketchbook, writing desk and drills come
  first. External instructions name free apps:
  - Art: Autodesk Sketchbook, MediBang or ibisPaint;
  - Editing: DaVinci Resolve for iPad, CapCut or Alight Motion;
  - Design: Figma or Canva.

  Procreate, Clip Studio, Affinity and LumaFusion are optional upgrades,
  offered only after a habit (§9.1).
- **Light and secondary.** The budget is 20–30 minutes a day, or a couple
  of sessions a week, strictly behind the exams, Code and Japanese. The
  Daily Creative pauses in Exam Season (§5.1).
- **First release:** the platform, the Writing Desk, Graphic design's
  interactive drills, and Art units 1–3 once the iPad's ink engine is
  ready (§10).

Short answer:
- **One subject, Creatives, with four tracks:** Art, Graphic design,
  Editing (video) and Writing. It is a non-exam subject with its own hue,
  desk and lessons on the shared topic page, like Code and Japanese.
- **What it is for:** a hobby and a way to make things for the media you
  love:
  - anime and light-novel characters, and background scenery;
  - short edits and AMVs;
  - clean UI and covers;
  - eventually, your own VN or light-novel scenes.
- **The method is deliberate practice in small, safe steps.** Every lesson
  ends in something made, usually a micro-study that isolates one skill.
  Drills are disposable. Only what you choose goes on show.
- **Kurenai never makes the work.** AI is a critic you ask, never an
  artist. It sees only what you send, and judges only what the unit
  taught.

## 1. Why this is not "Code with pictures"

Code proves skill by running tests (invariant 107). A drawing has no test
suite, so Creatives needs its own idea of evidence:

| Code | Creatives |
|---|---|
| A task passes its cases | A drill or micro-study is done to its brief and saved to the Practice Log. Its check is the unit's ONE skill, marked against "Good for this unit". |
| Retrieval cards from runnable variations | Theory cards (colour, composition, type, craft terms) on FSRS, plus "redo it" items: the same exercise weeks later, beside the old one |
| A capstone with acceptance cases | A **piece**: a brief and milestones, self-marked, never locked, and offered only after its micro-studies |
| The Rider validator | **Portfolio import:** an image, a video, a text or a link, with its time and tools |

Visible progress matters most here, and so does who sees it. Compare
yourself only with your own earlier work (the redo, §5.3), never with a
finished anime frame.

## 2. The four tracks

Each track is a **core path** of short units (fundamentals, in order).
Every unit is made of **micro-studies** (§3.1), and the **projects** come
only at the end of a stage. Sizes assume 20–30 minutes a day.

### 2.1 Art (the iPad's track)

**Core path:**
1. lines and confidence (ghosting, straight lines, ellipses);
2. boxes and forms (construction, rotation);
3. perspective (one and two point; three later);
4. value (light and shadow on simple forms);
5. colour basics (hue, value, saturation, palettes from a frame);
6. gesture;
7. the mannequin and proportions;
8. heads and the anime face;
9. hands;
10. clothing and folds;
11. backgrounds and scenery (perspective rooms, skies, nature);
12. composition;
13. a digital workflow (layers, flats, a line over a sketch).

**Micro-studies before pieces.** For example:
- trace box and cylinder construction OVER a screenshot or reference
  before freehanding it;
- copy one anime eye ten times;
- one background in one-point perspective with three objects;
- value-only thumbnails of a scene, no line.

**Projects** (only after their stage):
- a character bust in three expressions (before a full turnaround);
- one fan-art illustration;
- a light-novel-style cover (with Design's type);
- a one-page, four-panel manga (before a short);
- a VN sprite with two expressions.

### 2.2 Graphic design (no external app to start)

**Core path:**
- the principles (contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity, hierarchy);
- typography (anatomy, pairing, kerning, leading, the measure);
- grids and layout;
- colour in design;
- logos and marks;
- covers and posters;
- UI and screens (KurenaiOS's own design system as a case study);
- export.

**Interactive drills, in the app:**
- **kerning:** space a word by eye, then see the reference overlaid;
- **pairing:** pick a heading and body font for a brief, then see why;
- **hierarchy:** fix a flat layout's sizes and weights;
- **palette:** build a scheme that passes the contrast check;
- **grid:** snap blocks to a 12-column grid.

**Micro-studies, then projects:**
- recreate one existing cover's type in Figma or Canva (a study, not
  original) before designing one;
- a title for a Collection book or show;
- a poster;
- a mini brand for a fictional studio;
- one redesigned screen.

### 2.3 Editing (video first)

**Core path:**
- the cut and continuity;
- cutting on the beat;
- pacing;
- sound and music levels;
- a story in 30 seconds;
- colour correction, then a simple grade;
- titles and text;
- export for platforms.

**Micro-studies before an AMV:**
- **a 10–15 second, 8-beat loop:** eight cuts on eight beats from one
  episode;
- one match cut;
- one speed ramp;
- a 30-second sequence with a single mood.

**Projects:**
- a 30–45 second edit;
- then a full AMV;
- a trailer for a Collection title.

**In the app:**
- a **beat map** (tap along to a song; the markers export as a text list
  for Resolve or CapCut);
- **storyboard** frames in the sketchbook;
- shot lists;
- the theory cards.

The editing itself happens in DaVinci Resolve for iPad (free), CapCut or
Alight Motion. **Photo** is an optional later module.

### 2.4 Writing (the Writing Desk; no external app)

**Core path:**
- the sentence and the paragraph;
- point of view and voice;
- dialogue (and how VNs and LNs carry it);
- description and the senses;
- the scene;
- character (want, need, flaw);
- structure (three acts, kishōtenketsu, the light-novel volume);
- revision;
- forms: flash fiction, the short story, the VN script.

**Micro-studies before chapters.** For example:
- **a 150-word single dialogue exchange** (two characters, one want each);
- one room described through one sense;
- one paragraph rewritten three ways (first person, third limited,
  present tense);
- one scene of 300 words with a turn.

**Projects:**
- flash fiction (under 1,000 words);
- a short story through two drafts;
- a branching VN scene (a small script format the app previews);
- one light-novel chapter.

**The Writing Desk:**
- a distraction-free Markdown editor with word count and versions;
- a diff between drafts, so revision is visible;
- **sprints** in Focus (a word target and a timer);
- **reading as a writer:** pick a book or VN you finished from the
  Collection and answer the lesson's craft questions about it.

## 3. A lesson

### 3.1 Micro-studies (the taste-gap guardrail)

- **A micro-study isolates ONE variable,** with the other 80% given:
  - a reference to trace over;
  - a fixed palette;
  - a pre-cut clip and a beat grid;
  - a scene's setup already written.
  - Each one is 5–20 minutes.
- **A unit's micro-studies come first.** Its project is offered only when
  they are done, and every project milestone is itself
  micro-study-sized.
- **Big projects are locked behind small ones by content, never by
  rewards.** A "Try it anyway" link stays, because nothing is ever locked
  for good (as with a capstone).

### 3.2 The parts

1. **Notes:** short pages (the notes layout rule: lines and bullets, with
   annotated `{fig}` diagrams), and step images for every demonstration
   (for example "a box in four steps").
2. **The micro-studies:** what to make, how long, and the one skill
   checked.
3. **The check:** one or two rows, the unit's skill only, each against
   "Good for this unit" (§3.3). Each row gets a tick and, optionally, a
   sentence.
4. **Theory cards** (FSRS).
5. **Optional: ask Kurenai** (§7).

### 3.3 "Good for this unit" (calibration)

Every rubric row says plainly what a pass looks like at THIS unit, and
what does not matter yet. For example:

> **Unit 2 · Boxes.** Your lines will wobble, and nothing will look like
> a finished anime frame yet. Pass if the box's edges head to consistent
> vanishing points and the construction lines wrap the form. Ignore
> shading, line quality and proportions: they are later units.

Every lesson's notes also show an **"expected beginner output"** example:
a deliberately rough pass, never a polished one, so the bar is visible.

**Status from evidence,** as in Code (invariant 107):
- notes read;
- the micro-studies saved;
- the check done;
- a theory card proven.

Reading alone never completes a lesson.

## 4. The Practice Log and the Portfolio

Two places, kept apart on purpose.

- **The Practice Log (the sketchpad)** holds every drill and micro-study,
  raw.
  - It is **hidden by default.** It opens only from a lesson, the redo
    comparison or "Show my progress".
  - Nothing in it is ever shown on the desk, in Reports or to Kurenai
    unless you open it.
  - It can be pruned freely.
- **The Portfolio** holds only what you **choose** to promote: a piece,
  or a drill you are proud of.
  - It is the only gallery Reports or a share can show.
  - Promotion is a separate, deliberate tap, never automatic.
- **Batches and best-of-five.** A timed drill saves as ONE batch (for
  example 20 gestures or 100 boxes). Afterwards you star the one best rep
  in each five (optional) and critique only the starred ones. The rest
  stay in the batch, uncritiqued.
- **Momentum mode (no eraser).** In timed sketchbook drills, an optional
  switch removes undo and the eraser:
  - every line is committed;
  - the timer moves to the next rep;
  - the batch is marked "Momentum".
  - It is off for studies and pieces, and remembered per drill type.
- **Storage:** a new IndexedDB, `kurenai-os-creatives`, for the files.
  The records (titles, dates, check ticks, links) are small and sync.
  Files upload only when you say so, like topic attachments (invariant
  36, decision F).
- **Bringing work in:** "Add to Practice Log" or "Add to Portfolio" from
  a file (an image, a video, a text). A share from another app straight
  into the installed app is checked in Spike C.
- **Hours** come from Focus: a Creatives session links a track and a
  lesson, like a study session, and a mid-way switch works (invariant 4a).

## 5. Practice and scheduling

### 5.1 The day

A **Daily Creative** is computed from the evidence and sized to the
budget (20–30 minutes):
- one drill, OR one micro-study, OR a project milestone;
- the due theory cards.

It is never stored, and it is the same all day.
- It skips days without guilt: a "couple of sessions a week" setting
  makes it appear on chosen days only, with no streak pressure.
- It **pauses in Exam Season** (invariant 123, on by default) and says so
  in one line.

### 5.2 Theory on FSRS

Theory cards are ordinary FSRS cards (invariant 111), keyed
`creatives:<lesson>:<i>` and scheduled through `KOS.srs.weights()`.
Nothing new.

### 5.3 The redo

A drill or micro-study marked "redo" comes back on an FSRS interval (weeks
and months, not days). You do it again with the old attempt hidden, then
the two show side by side and you rate the redo. The comparison is always
you against you, the only comparison the app makes.

### 5.4 References

Four sources, in this order:
- **The procedural reference generator (zero setup).** It draws in the
  app with no files and no copyright:
  - boxes, cylinders, spheres and cones at random angles;
  - a one- or two-point perspective grid;
  - a simple block mannequin (head, ribcage, pelvis, limbs) in a random
    pose;
  - a block head turned at random.
  - Each has "next", "show construction" and a seed, so a drill can be
    repeated. It runs on the lab system's SVG stage (`KOS.lab`), so it is
    themed and needs nothing new.
- **Your own references:** an imported folder or photos you take.
- **Collection covers and frames** for private colour and composition
  studies. They are never exported with a piece.
- **Tracing-over mode:** any reference can sit under the sketchbook at
  30% for the tracing micro-studies.

Nothing is hot-linked from a stock site and nothing is bundled.

## 6. Rewards (the Governor)

- **Creatives is study.** A linked Focus session pays by the Focus rules
  (invariant 4a). It is not leisure (invariant 3).
- **Proven evidence pays once:**
  - a micro-study with its check: 8 XP and 1 gold;
  - a drill batch: 6 XP (one a day per drill type);
  - a theory card proven: +4 XP, within the shared daily cap;
  - a project with every milestone: like a stage-1 capstone (150 XP,
    40 gold, +10 HP).
  - Redoing, re-saving, promoting or asking for a critique pays nothing
    more.
- **Volume pays nothing by itself** (words, strokes, reps, minutes), so
  quantity is never farmed.
- **No streak pressure:** Creatives days count for the study streak like
  any study, but missing one never costs anything Creatives-specific.

## 7. Kurenai's role (decision E)

- **A critique on request:** one request after a "What is sent" dialog
  (invariant 102b). It carries the starred image or excerpt, the brief,
  the **unit number and its one skill**, and the "Good for this unit"
  text.
  - The prompt tells it to judge ONLY that skill at that level, to ignore
    every later skill (shading during a perspective drill, anatomy during
    gesture), and never to compare with professional work.
  - The reply is what worked against the unit's bar, and ONE thing to try
    on the next rep.
- **Never:** generating images, rewriting prose or finishing anything.
  The Assistant has read tools (`creatives_overview`,
  `creatives_portfolio`; the Practice Log only when asked) and no write
  tool for evidence (as with Code, invariant 110).

## 8. Joins with the rest of the app

- **Goals:** drill days, micro-studies done, words written and Creatives
  hours (invariant 5b), read from the log and Focus.
- **Reports:** `f.creatives` holds totals only (invariant 102b). A "made
  this month" strip shows PORTFOLIO thumbnails only, when you turn it on.
  The Practice Log never appears.
- **The Collection:** "made from" links a piece to the title it is fan art
  of, an edit to its anime, a scene to the VN behind it.
- **Japanese:** a VN sprite project can make art for its cast (that plan's
  §12.1).
- **Calendar:** a project's milestones can be dated and drawn as derived
  chips (invariant 44).

## 9. The iPad and the sketchbook

The sketchbook is built on the iPad plan's ink engine (6.1) and adds:
- **Layers-lite:** a sketch layer at 30%, ink and colour. Three layers,
  opacity, hide, and merge down.
- **A few brushes:** pencil, ink (tapering), marker, flat colour and
  eraser. Width comes from speed and tilt (Metapen) or pressure (Pencil
  Pro).
- **Drill tools:**
  - a rep timer that saves and clears between reps;
  - Momentum mode (§4);
  - the reference panel and the procedural generator (§5.4);
  - tracing-over at 30%;
  - a mirror flip and a greyscale preview.
- **Export** to PNG, to open in a free app (Autodesk Sketchbook, MediBang,
  ibisPaint) for anything bigger.

It stays a drills-and-studies tool.

### 9.1 Free first, upgrades later

- External steps name free apps only. Each track's settings list the
  paid upgrades (Procreate and Clip Studio for Art, LumaFusion for
  Editing, Affinity for Design) as "Optional, once the habit sticks".
- That note appears after **20 sessions in the track**, never before, and
  no lesson ever depends on a paid app.

## 14. Invariants this would add (draft, numbered after the iPad's)

- **134. CREATIVE EVIDENCE IS MADE, NOT CLAIMED.**
  - A lesson's checks come from saved work, its single-skill check and
    proven theory cards, written only by the Creatives core.
  - Each proof pays once.
  - Volume pays nothing beyond Focus.
- **135. KURENAI CRITIQUES, NEVER CREATES.**
  - No tool generates or rewrites a piece.
  - A critique sends only what "What is sent" shows, once per press.
  - It is judged against the unit's one skill and its "Good for this
    unit" text.
- **136. PRACTICE IS PRIVATE, THE PORTFOLIO IS CHOSEN.**
  - The Practice Log is hidden unless opened, and is never shown by
    Reports, a share or Kurenai.
  - Only a deliberate promotion puts work in the Portfolio.
  - Files are local first and upload only on request.
  - References are generated or the user's own.

## 15. Risks

- **The taste gap still bites.** The defences are:
  - micro-studies keep each attempt small;
  - calibration says what a pass looks like;
  - the redo shows progress against yourself;
  - the Practice Log keeps drills off display;
  - critique is unit-bound.
- **Too wide.** Four disciplines can each fill years. Units stay short,
  and release one set at a time.
- **Time against the exams.** 20–30 minutes, chosen days only, and the
  automatic Exam Season pause.
- **Free apps change.** Instructions name the step ("import, cut on the
  marker"), not menus, so a different free app still works.
