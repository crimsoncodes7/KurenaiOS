# Psychology & Philosophy — a study space for the mind (plan, revision 1, for review)

Roadmap 5.9, asked for on 24 October 2026. It follows the shape of Code,
Japanese and Creatives: a plan first, then Claude Design
([prompt 16](design-prompts.md), still to write), then a platform
release, then one unit per release.

Short answer:
- **One subject with two disciplines.** It is called Psychology &
  Philosophy, or "Mind" for short (decision A). It is not an exam
  subject. It gets its own hue, desk and lessons on the shared topic
  page, like Code and Japanese, and sits in their subject group.
- **15 minutes a day:** reviews, one lesson sitting and one question to
  think about. It shrinks to 5 minutes of reviews during Exam Season.
- **The concept is the atom.** Paths contain units, units contain
  lessons, and lessons teach **concepts**. A concept is what FSRS
  schedules, what the knowledge graph links, and what mastery measures.
- **Philosophy is argued, not memorised.** Every lesson ends with your
  **position** on a question. The Journal keeps every version of it, so
  you can see your views change over months.
- **Psychology is tested on yourself.** Classic experiments (Stroop,
  serial position, anchoring) run as labs on your own data. The app's own
  mechanics (FSRS, streaks, the Governor) are taught as case studies:
  KurenaiOS explains itself.
- **The companion is a hybrid** (decision E). Authored Socratic dialogue
  trees are the default: free, offline and the same every time. "Ask
  Kurenai" goes further when you press it, and it only sends what
  "What is sent" shows.

## 1. Why this is not "CS notes with essays"

The other spaces prove skill with something checkable: a test passes, a
reading is right, a piece is made. Here most of what matters is
**understanding and judgement**, so the evidence is layered:

| Level | What it means | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Recall** | You can state the idea | FSRS concept cards (invariant 111) |
| **Explain** | You can say it in your own words | An explain-back, checked against the concept's key points (§3.3) |
| **Apply** | You can spot it in a new case | Scenario items: "which concept is this?" or "what would X say?" |
| **Evaluate** | You can weigh it | A position with a reason and the strongest objection answered (§6) |

A concept's **mastery** is the highest level whose evidence holds, and it
is derived, never set (like invariant 107). Recall decays with FSRS. The
other three are kept as dated evidence, and the "Revisit" queue (§5.3)
brings them back.

## 2. The curriculum

### 2.1 Paths

**Paths → units → lessons → concepts.** There are three kinds of path:

**Foundation (first, and required before the others):**
- **F. Thinking well:** arguments and their structure, validity and
  soundness, deduction and induction, the common fallacies, evidence and
  base rates, how to read a study, the principle of charity and
  steelmanning. Every later lesson uses these tools.

**Philosophy:**
- **P1. Ethics:** virtue ethics, utilitarianism, Kant and duty, contract
  theory, care ethics, applied cases, and metaethics later.
- **P2. Mind and self:** dualism, physicalism, functionalism,
  consciousness and the hard problem, personal identity, free will.
- **P3. Knowledge and reality:** scepticism, justification, Gettier,
  induction's problem, what exists, and time.
- **P4. How to live:** Stoicism, Epicureanism, existentialism and
  absurdism, and East Asian thought (Confucius, Zhuangzi, Zen, and
  Japanese aesthetics: *mono no aware*, *wabi-sabi*, *ikigai* as it is
  actually used rather than as the Venn diagram).
- **P5. Society and power** (later): justice, rights, liberty and
  authority.

**Psychology:**
- **Y1. Learning and memory** (first, because it is the most useful):
  encoding, the forgetting curve, the spacing and testing effects,
  interleaving, sleep, attention and multitasking, and metacognition.
- **Y2. Social psychology:** conformity, obedience, attribution,
  persuasion, groups, and the replication crisis told honestly.
- **Y3. Biases and decisions:** heuristics, framing, anchoring, loss
  aversion, sunk costs, and the dual-process debate.
- **Y4. Emotion, motivation and habit:** theories of emotion,
  self-determination theory, habit loops, rewards and intrinsic
  motivation, and flow.
- **Y5. Development and personality:** attachment, the Big Five,
  identity, and what changes with age.
- **Y6. Research methods:** designs, variables, sampling, ethics,
  effect sizes and p-values. It links to Maths' Statistics (S1–S5).
- **Y7. Mind and brain** (later): neurons, the brain's systems, and what
  imaging can and cannot show.

**Crossings** (a unit owned by both disciplines, offered once you have
done a unit on each side):
- **X1. Free will:** Libet and its critics ↔ compatibilism.
- **X2. Morality:** moral psychology (Haidt, trolley-problem data) ↔
  normative ethics.
- **X3. Happiness:** eudaimonia ↔ positive psychology and hedonic
  adaptation.
- **X4. The self:** personal identity ↔ self-concept and memory.
- **X5. Machine minds:** the Chinese room and functionalism ↔ what
  language models do. It links to CS and Code.

### 2.2 The suggested order

1. F. Thinking well (units 1–2);
2. Y1. Learning and memory (it pays back in every other subject);
3. P1. Ethics, unit 1 (see the example below);
4. X3. Happiness (the first crossing);
5. after that, your choice. The desk always suggests one next unit
   (§5.1) and never locks anything except crossings, which need their
   two sides first. Even that is a suggestion you can override.

### 2.3 Example units

These show the size, not a final list. A unit has 6–10 lessons, and a
lesson takes one or two 8-minute sittings.

**F1. What makes an argument good**
1. Claims, reasons and conclusions
2. Standard form: writing an argument out
3. Valid is not the same as true
4. Deduction and induction
5. Five fallacies you will meet weekly
6. The principle of charity
7. Steelmanning: making the other side stronger
8. Checkpoint: map and judge a real argument

**Y1. How memory works (and why your flashcards do)**
1. Encoding, storage and retrieval
2. Ebbinghaus and the forgetting curve (with your own review log)
3. The testing effect
4. The spacing effect, and what FSRS does about it
5. Interleaving and desirable difficulties
6. Sleep and consolidation
7. Attention and the myth of multitasking
8. Metacognition: knowing what you know (with your own calibration data)
9. Checkpoint: design your own revision week from the evidence

**P1. Living well: Aristotle and the virtues** (after your example)
1. What is ethics for?
2. *Eudaimonia*: flourishing, not feeling good
3. The function argument
4. Virtue as a trained character
5. The doctrine of the mean
6. Practical wisdom (*phronesis*)
7. Friendship
8. Objections: whose virtues? and the action-guiding problem
9. Synthesis: does Aristotle explain what makes a life good?

### 2.4 Content rules

- **Original writing.** Primary sources are quoted only from
  public-domain translations (Ross's Aristotle, Jowett's Plato, Long's
  Marcus Aurelius), cited every time. Modern works are summarised and
  cited, never copied.
- **Psychology claims carry their evidence.** Each finding is marked
  **Robust**, **Debated** or **Failed to replicate** (Stanford prison,
  ego depletion and power posing get the honest version).
- **Notes follow the house style:** short lines, bullets, and annotated
  `{fig}` diagrams wherever a picture helps (argument maps, curves,
  brain diagrams).
- **No therapy.** Applied units (Stoic practice, recognising thinking
  traps) are educational. They say so once and point to real help.
  Nothing diagnoses or assesses you.

## 3. A lesson

### 3.1 The pages

A lesson is short, with 4–6 pages on the shared topic page:
1. **Hook.** A puzzle, a thought experiment, or a quick vote ("Would you
   plug into the experience machine?"). Your answer is kept so the end
   of the lesson can return to it.
2. **The idea.** The concept or concepts, in short lines and with a
   diagram.
3. **The case.** A worked example, a study, or a passage read closely.
4. **The pushback.** The strongest objection, or the evidence against.
5. **Check.** 3–5 quiz items, including at least one "apply" scenario.
6. **Where you stand.** Your position (§6) and a closing Socratic
   dialogue (§7.1). It ends with the quick reference.

### 3.2 Concepts

A concept is authored once and reused everywhere:
```
{ id: "eudaimonia", name, gist (one line), keyPoints [3–5],
  cards [[q, a], …], scenarios [...], links [{to, type}], sources [...] }
```
Its id is stable, so cards, graph edges and journal links survive a
rewrite (like invariant 87).

### 3.3 The explain-back

"Explain *the doctrine of the mean* to a friend in three sentences."
You write it, then tick which key points you covered against the
concept's list. That self-check is the evidence. "Ask Kurenai to check
it" is optional and advisory: it never sets mastery (decision F).

## 4. Progress and milestones

- **A lesson** has four checks, derived from evidence: pages read, check
  passed, position recorded, and its concepts' first retrieval passed.
  The status follows the checks, as Code's does (invariant 107).
- **A unit** ends in a **checkpoint**. It mixes concept cards, apply
  scenarios, and one short written synthesis that you mark against a
  rubric. It is never locked and can be retaken.
- **Milestones** show on the desk and in Reports:
  - first argument mapped;
  - ten positions taken;
  - first crossing unit;
  - first changed mind (a position revised with a reason, §6);
  - first self-experiment finished (§8.1).
- **Mastery** per concept (Recall → Evaluate) and per path appears as a
  ladder, not a percentage.

## 5. The daily session (15 minutes)

### 5.1 The Daily 15

It is computed from the date and the evidence and never stored, like
Code's Daily 20 (invariant 108):

| Minutes | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| ~4 | **Reviews** | Due concept cards (FSRS), capped at 15 a day |
| ~8 | **One sitting** | The next part of the current lesson; a lesson is cut into sittings at page breaks |
| ~3 | **One question** | The week's Big Question (§9.2), a revisit (§5.3), or a lesson's Socratic close |

- It is one Focus session, so it pays as study through the Governor's
  normal rule (invariant 4a). A 15-minute session earns one block.
- **Short day:** "Just reviews" (5 minutes) is always offered.
- **Exam Season** (invariant 123) shrinks it to reviews only, without
  being asked. Lessons wait, and nothing reads as behind.
- **No streak of its own.** It feeds the normal study streak only. A
  missed day carries nothing over and nothing goes overdue except cards,
  which FSRS already handles.

### 5.2 Retrieval

- Concept cards schedule through `KOS.fsrs` with the learner's weights
  and target retention (invariant 111), keyed `mind:<concept>:<i>`. The
  review log records `src: "mind"`.
- Only cards **worth keeping** are authored: the definition, the key
  distinction ("valid vs sound"), the classic objection, and the finding
  and its status. Names and dates are rarely worth a card.
- Mixed reviews can show one concept's card next to its **look-alike**
  (utilitarianism vs consequentialism, negative reinforcement vs
  punishment). Each concept lists its confusables.

### 5.3 Revisits

Positions and explain-backs come back too, but not as cards. Spaced at
roughly 2 weeks, 2 months and 6 months, a revisit asks: "In October you
said X because Y. Still true?" You keep it, revise it, or retire it.
That is what powers the belief timeline (§6).

## 6. Positions: where you stand (novel)

On each Big Question a lesson raises ("Can a machine think?", "Is
lying ever right?"):
- your answer, from a scale or a set of options;
- your confidence, from 50% to 99%;
- your reason, in one or two sentences;
- the best objection and your reply. This is optional, but you need it
  to reach **Evaluate**.

Positions are **never overwritten**. Each revision is a new dated
version with "what changed my mind". The **belief timeline** shows how
your views drift, and a revision counts as a milestone, because
changing your mind for a reason is the point.

**The consistency check.** Authored tension pairs flag answers that pull
against each other, for example "You pull the lever in the trolley case
but don't push in the footbridge case. What's the difference?" The pair
opens a short dialogue instead of a verdict. Tensions are authored, never
guessed by AI.

## 7. The companion

### 7.1 The dialogue trees (the default)

Each lesson carries a short authored **Socratic dialogue**:
- Kurenai asks a question;
- you pick from 2–4 answers or type your own, and a typed answer is
  matched to the nearest branch by keywords, with "none of these" as a
  way out;
- each branch has a written follow-up, a counterexample or a deeper
  question;
- after 3–5 turns it ends in a short summary of the line you took.

Why this is the default:
- it works offline, costs nothing and teaches the same way every time;
- every branch can be reviewed for accuracy;
- it can be voiced and drawn: it reuses the VN scene component
  (`KOS.jaUI.scene`) with Kurenai's existing art, and needs no new cast.

### 7.2 Ask Kurenai (optional)

A button on every lesson, concept and journal entry. There are four
modes, each a fixed prompt to `KOS.ai.chat`:

| Mode | What Kurenai does |
|---|---|
| **Socratic** | Asks only questions and never gives the answer until you ask for it |
| **Explain it differently** | A simpler or more concrete explanation, then one check question |
| **Push back** | Argues the strongest case against your position: a steelman, not a strawman |
| **Make it mine** | Examples drawn from what you allow: your subjects, a Collection title you choose, your week |

- **"What is sent"** lists the payload before the first message, as in
  Reports and Creatives: the concept, the lesson, your typed position,
  and only the extras you tick. Journal entries never go unless you send
  one by hand.
- A conversation can be **saved to the Journal** as a reflection.
- It never sets mastery, writes a position or grades a checkpoint.
- **Assistant tools** are read-only: `mind_overview`, `mind_concept` and
  `mind_due`. Saving to the Journal goes through the approval card.

## 8. Psychology on yourself (novel)

### 8.1 Self-experiments in the lab

Classic demonstrations are built on the lab system (`KOS.lab`, the text
and SVG kinds, never a canvas):
- Stroop;
- serial position (free recall of 15 words);
- digit span;
- anchoring;
- change blindness;
- reaction time with and without a distractor;
- the Müller-Lyer illusion.

Each produces your own result and the published one, side by side. Y6
then teaches you to test the difference with the statistics you are
learning in Maths. Results stay on the device.

### 8.2 KurenaiOS explains itself

Lessons use your own app data as their case study. These are pure reads
with the low-data rule (invariant 76):
- **the forgetting curve** from your review log (`KOS.reviewLog`);
- **calibration** from your exam predictions (`KOS.examStats`);
- **time of day:** your Focus completion by hour;
- **rewards:** how the Governor uses reinforcement, why HP gates
  nothing (invariant 2), and what research says about streaks.

This makes the app's design choices visible and open to argument.

## 9. The Journal

### 9.1 Entries

Five kinds, each linked to concepts and, optionally, to a lesson, a
topic, or a Collection title:

| Kind | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Insight** | Something that clicked |
| **Quote** | Short, with its source; your own collection, never bundled |
| **Question** | Something open; it can become a Big Question |
| **Position** | §6: versioned, with confidence |
| **Reflection** | Free writing, or a saved Kurenai conversation |

- Search, filter by kind or concept, and a "this month" view.
- A **commonplace book** export (Markdown) of everything, or one
  concept's entries.
- **Private by default.** Reports count entries only and never quote
  them (invariant 102b's rule). Kurenai sees an entry only when you send
  it.

### 9.2 The Big Question of the week

One question each week, chosen from your open Questions or the current
unit:
- a 3-minute prompt on most days builds a short thread;
- on Sunday, a 100-word synthesis closes it.

### 9.3 Storage (decision G)

The Journal is **text you write and want to keep**, so it does not
belong in the state document:
- its own IndexedDB, `kurenai-os-mind`;
- included in the full backup;
- replicated to an owner-only `kos_mind` table the way Money and the
  review log are (local first, per-row stamps, tombstones).

Lesson evidence and positions' metadata stay in `state.mind` as keyed
maps.

## 10. The knowledge graph

- **Nodes:** concepts, plus thinkers and studies as light nodes.
- **Typed edges:** *is a kind of*, *objects to*, *supports*,
  *is evidence for*, *same idea as*, *applies in*.
- **Edges out of the space** use links the app already has:
  - **CS and Maths** through topic refs (`"sid:ref"`, invariant 26e).
    For example: logic ↔ Boolean algebra, induction ↔ proof by
    induction, the Chinese room ↔ the Turing machine and computability,
    base rates ↔ conditional probability, and research methods ↔
    hypothesis testing;
  - **Code** concepts (X5, recursion as self-reference);
  - **Japanese** lessons (P4's aesthetics, honorifics as social
    psychology);
  - **Collection titles** by `syncId`, private. This is the "media lens":
    you tag a title with the ideas it explores (determinism in
    Steins;Gate, identity in Ghost in the Shell, the hedgehog's dilemma
    in Evangelion). A few are suggested; the rest are yours.
- **Your edges.** A journal entry's links add your own edges, drawn
  apart from the authored ones.
- **Views:**
  - each concept page has a **Neighbours** panel, which is the
    accessible route;
  - the **Atlas** is an SVG map of one path or one concept's
    two-step neighbourhood, built on the lab stage and its overflow
    rule. Like the braid (invariant 85), the picture is one image and
    the list beneath it is the real control.
- `KOS.search` finds concepts, thinkers and journal entries, but never
  journal text in the cross-domain list unless you are on the Journal.

## 11. Other ideas (recommended, in rough order of value)

1. **The Argument Mapper.** Drag premises and a conclusion into a tree
   with *because*, *but* and *therefore*. Used in F and in every
   checkpoint, and exportable to the Journal. It is mostly a drag-list
   (`KOS.ui.reorder`) with indentation, so it is cheap.
2. **The Fallacy Field Guide.** Short daily items: a quote or a fake ad,
   and you name the fallacy or bias. You can log "spotted in the wild"
   entries from your own life to the Journal.
3. **Thought-experiment theatre.** About ten classics as interactive VN
   scenes: the trolley, the experience machine, Mary's room, the ship of
   Theseus, the veil of ignorance, the Chinese room, Newcomb's problem.
   Your choices feed the consistency check (§6), and the results page
   shows where your answers place you ("you answered like a
   consequentialist 4 times out of 6").
4. **Philosopher cards.** A small collectable deck: a thinker, the one
   idea, an objection. A card is "earned" when you reach Apply on that
   thinker's main concept. It is display only and pays nothing.
5. **The Stoic evening review.** An optional 2-minute prompt at night:
   what went well, what was in your control, what to do differently. It
   could sit beside the Governor's recovery checklist. It is never
   scored.
6. **Debate rounds with Kurenai.** A timed exchange: you argue, Kurenai
   argues back, you rebut. A self-marked rubric (charity, evidence, the
   objection answered) closes it. Built on §7.2 "Push back".
7. **The reading room.** Short public-domain passages (Meditations,
   the Apology, Nicomachean Ethics II) with guided margin notes and
   questions, a few paragraphs at a sitting.
8. **Annual "what I believe".** On 1 September, Reports' year view
   (invariant 102d) shows your positions then and now. It counts
   facts only and shows your words only when you open it.

## 12. Rewards (the Governor)

- The Daily 15 is a Focus session and pays under the Focus rule
  (invariant 4a). That is the main route.
- **Pay once:**
  - a lesson's first finish: 12 XP, 2 gold, +2 HP;
  - a unit checkpoint: 50 XP, 12 gold, +4 HP;
  - a path complete: 120 XP, 30 gold, +8 HP.
- A proven concept review pays +1 XP, at most 15 a day (like Japanese,
  invariant 116).
- Journal entries, positions, dialogues, Kurenai, self-experiments and
  philosopher cards pay **nothing**. Writing more must never earn more.
- HP gates nothing (invariant 2). Nothing in the space is locked behind
  gold.

## 17. Risks

- **Too much on the plate.** This is the fourth daily space. The
  defences: 15 minutes, the 5-minute "just reviews" day, the automatic
  Exam Season shrink, and no streak of its own.
- **Philosophy turning into memorising.** Every lesson ends in a
  position, and cards are limited to what is worth keeping.
- **Pop-psychology errors.** Each claim is labelled with its evidence,
  failed replications are taught openly, and sources are cited.
- **The content is large.** Units stay short, one ships per release, and
  the trees have a checker.
- **Applied units mistaken for therapy.** They are educational, say so,
  and never assess you.
